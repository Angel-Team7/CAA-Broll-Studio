Roast of the roast

The previous proposal was directionally right and structurally soft. Judged against what it must deliver, here is what survives.

Keep
Shot lists per beat

The single biggest lever. Every probe confirmed it: the same sources return on-beat footage the moment the query is a filmable shot in the brand's world.

Keep
Claude looks at the frames before anything is downloaded

Pexels hands over fifteen frames per clip in the search result. Pixabay's tiny file yields three. Judging on pictures is the only step that catches chickens, masks, glaciers and factories.

Cull
SigLIP embedding pre-rank in the Action

A gigabyte of PyTorch in CI to save the judge a few images. The judge can scan a twelve-up grid of posters in one look and do the same job with no dependency. Revisit only if judge volume ever becomes the bottleneck.

Cull
Four-hop chain: Action, routine, Action, cockpit

Every trigger boundary has already bitten us this week. Verified today: the webhook fires the judge routine on any push to the repo, and it accepts no path filter. So the design has two hops, the routine exits in seconds when nothing is waiting, and the cockpit degrades gracefully if the judge is late.

Cull
Embeddings as memory

Without SigLIP there are no vectors. Memory becomes what Claude reads natively: captions and scores on every judged clip, a global rejected ledger by source id, and a contact sheet of the brand's latest approvals shown to the judge as reference.

Change
Fetch 40, rank, show 10

Right shape, wrong numbers and no diversity. Recall 150 by metadata, shortlist 24 by a cheap text pass, build previews for those 24 in the same Action run, judge them, show the top 10 ranked with captions and keep the rest one click away. No shoot contributes more than two.

Change
Haiku through an API key

Replaced by Sonnet inside a Claude routine on the plan. Stronger judge, no key, no bill. The routine reads contact sheets with its own eyes and commits the ranking.

Keep
Honest failure

Fewer than three candidates clear the bar, the beat is marked "stock cannot show this" with the judge's reason and routes to generated visuals. Junk padding ends.

Claude Code native principles

These are the rules the plan is built on. They are what made the last two days' fixes stick, written down so every session, cloud or Mac, works the same way.

  1. The repo is the brain.Taste laws, brand worlds and the pipeline map live in the repo's CLAUDE.md. Every session that clones the repo inherits them without a prompt.
  2. Procedures are skills, not prompts.Shot-list, judge and intake are SKILL.md files under .claude/skills in the repo. A routine's prompt becomes one line. Versioned, diffable, reviewable, identical everywhere.
  3. Files over services.Candidates, verdicts, ledgers and the library are JSON in git. No database, no queue, nothing to log into.
  4. Keys stay in GitHub, judgement stays in Claude.Actions hold the stock keys and ffmpeg. Routines hold the eyes. Neither needs the other's secrets.
  5. Two hops maximum, every job idempotent.A job claims its work by moving a file, tags its commit, and can be re-run safely. A routine fired with nothing to do exits in seconds.
  6. Verify before depending.The worker was tested in Node, Safari in a WebKit harness, the webhook probed field by field before this page was written. Every new dependency gets the same treatment, and the harnesses live in tools/verify.
  7. Fail loudly, in the cockpit.Pipefail everywhere, run summaries on every job, and a status line on the card: "judged 3 min ago" or "24 candidates awaiting judge".
  8. Ticks are training data, never targets.Automation appends candidates and captions. It never touches an approval. Approvals feed the judge's reference sheet; Direction is wrong feeds the rejected ledger.
  9. Show, don't sniff.Every clip carries a caption of what it shows and why it ranked. The reviewer judges the judge.

The pipeline

Routine
Shot list

From the spoken line, the brief, the lesson and the brand world: four to six filmable shots, each with a natural phrasing for Pexels and Coverr and a tag phrasing for Pixabay, plus a must-not list. Stored on the card, shown under the brief, editable by the reviewer.

Action
Recall

Every phrasing against Pexels, Pixabay, Coverr, Wikimedia for real places, Unsplash and Openverse for stills. Landscape, 5 to 40 seconds, 1280 wide. Pexels titles derived from the page address. About 150 candidates, metadata and frames only.

Action
Shortlist and stage

A cheap text pass against the shot list and the rejected ledger cuts 150 to 24. For those 24 the Action builds the 480p preview, the thumb and a three-frame contact strip, uploads them, and commits a candidates file. Nothing appears on the card yet.

Routine
Judge

The push fires the judge routine. It reads the strips, the shot list, the spoken line, the must-not list and the brand's reference sheet of recent approvals. Per candidate: relevance and brand fit 0 to 10, a one-line caption, any violation. Then the diversity rule: no shoot more than twice, wide and close mixed, seven videos and three stills.

Routine
Deliver

Top ten written to the card in rank order with captions and a best-match badge; the remaining fourteen kept behind "show more". Every verdict appended to the library ledger so nothing is judged twice. Beats under three usable are marked "stock cannot show this".

Cockpit
Review

Ranked grid, caption under each clip, shot list under the brief, status line. Need more asks for the next shots on the list. Direction is wrong feeds every untouched clip to the rejected ledger and moves to shots not yet used.

Timing of a click. Recall and staging in the Action, about three minutes. Judge routine spin-up and verdict, about two. A ranked card in roughly five minutes, and an honest "awaiting judge" state with the unranked 24 available in the meantime.
